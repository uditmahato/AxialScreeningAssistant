# Government Health Support Schemes in Nepal

Cost is often the decisive factor in whether a Nepali patient completes a
referral. These schemes exist to reduce that barrier, but awareness of them is
low - including among health workers. Raising them proactively is a meaningful
clinical intervention.

## 1. National Health Insurance Programme (Swasthya Bima)

Administered by the Health Insurance Board.

- **Enrolment** is by household, at ward or local level
- **Premium**: commonly cited at around NPR 3,500 per year for a household of
  up to five members, with an additional per-member charge beyond that
- **Coverage**: an annual benefit ceiling per household, commonly cited at
  around NPR 100,000, rising with additional members
- **Ultra-poor households, and certain categories including senior citizens and
  people with severe disability**, may be enrolled with the premium subsidised
  or waived
- Services are obtained at empanelled facilities, with referral required for
  higher-level care

**Practical point:** the insurance only helps at *empanelled* facilities and
usually requires following the referral chain. A patient who self-refers to a
private hospital in Kathmandu typically cannot claim. Check empanelment before
travelling.

## 2. Bipanna Nagarik Aausadhi Upachar Kosh (Poor Citizens' Treatment Fund)

A treatment grant for specified serious conditions for citizens of limited
means.

- Covers a defined list of conditions which has included **cancer, head
  injury, spinal injury, heart disease, kidney disease, Alzheimer's and
  Parkinson's disease, and sickle cell anaemia**
- **Brain tumour treatment may be claimable under the cancer category**, and
  **head injury** is separately listed - both directly relevant here
- Support is commonly cited at up to around NPR 100,000, with higher ceilings
  for some categories
- Applied for through the treating hospital's social service unit, with
  recommendation from the local ward office

**Practical point:** ask the hospital's **social service unit** on arrival.
Applications generally have to be made through the hospital, and patients who
do not ask are frequently not offered.

## 3. National Tuberculosis Programme

**TB diagnosis and treatment are provided free of charge** through government
facilities and DOTS centres nationwide.

This is directly relevant to the brain-lesion differential in Nepal: if a
lesion proves to be a tuberculoma, the treatment itself is free, even though
the diagnostic imaging was not. Raising this early can change a family's
decision about whether to pursue investigation at all. See [[tuberculoma]] and
[[nepal-tb-programme]].

## 4. Senior citizen provisions

Citizens above a specified age (commonly cited as 70, with variations) are
entitled to support for treatment costs at government facilities. Requirements
and amounts vary; enquire locally.

## 5. Basic health services at government facilities

A defined package of basic health services is provided free at government
health posts and primary centres. Emergency stabilisation should not be refused
for inability to pay.

## 6. Local and provincial government support

Municipalities and provincial governments operate their own health support
funds, which vary considerably. The ward office is the point of enquiry, and
these are frequently overlooked.

## What a health worker should do

1. **Ask about the insurance card** at first contact
2. If not enrolled, explain how to enrol at the ward office
3. For a suspected serious diagnosis, **direct the family to the hospital's
   social service unit** before admission
4. Mention that **TB treatment is free**, where that is in the differential
5. Be realistic about total cost, including transport, food and accommodation
   for the family - which frequently exceeds the medical fee itself


> **Verify before relying on these figures.** Costs, entitlements and contact
> details in Nepal change frequently. The figures here are indicative, compiled
> on the review date above, and are intended to help a patient plan - not to
> quote as a guaranteed price. Confirm directly with the facility or scheme.
