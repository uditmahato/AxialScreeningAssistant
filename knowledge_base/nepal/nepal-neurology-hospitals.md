# Neurology and Neurosurgery Centres in Nepal

25 facilities with neurological or neurosurgical capability, by
province. Structured data for the application is in `hospitals.json`.

## The access problem in numbers

Nepal has **114 neurosurgeons registered with the Nepalese Society of
Neurosurgeons**, a density of **0.39 per 100,000 population** - roughly one per
256,000 people (Shrestha, 2024; Alokozay et al., 2025).

The commonly cited benchmark of **one neurosurgeon per 100,000** comes from the
American College of Surgeons and the American Association of Neurological
Surgeons, not from WHO. Against it, Nepal sits at about 39% of the target and
would need approximately 178 additional neurosurgeons to reach it, while
currently training around 11 per year.

> **Correction note.** Earlier drafts of this project, including the interim
> report, stated "30-40 neurosurgeons, one per 750,000, against a WHO target".
> Both halves were wrong: the workforce figure is roughly a third of the true
> count and predates 2016, and the benchmark is not WHO's. The error is
> recorded rather than quietly removed because the corrected figures still
> describe a serious access problem, and the correction itself is worth
> reporting.

Capability is heavily concentrated in the Kathmandu Valley. A patient in a
far-western or Karnali district may travel 8-12 hours to reach an MRI scanner,
and considerably further for neurosurgery.

## Choosing where to send a patient

**Emergency (stroke, head injury, reducing consciousness):** the nearest
facility with **CT** and an emergency department. Do not bypass a closer
CT-capable hospital to reach a neurosurgical centre - stabilisation and imaging
come first.

**Suspected tumour, non-urgent:** the nearest centre with neurosurgery and MRI.
For eastern Nepal, BPKIHS Dharan avoids the journey to Kathmandu. For the
western hills, Manipal Pokhara. For the central Terai, Bharatpur.

**Confirmed brain tumour needing radiotherapy or chemotherapy:** BPKMCH
Bharatpur, Bhaktapur Cancer Hospital, or Nepal Cancer Hospital.

**Cost-constrained patient:** government facilities - TUTH, Bir/NAMS, BPKIHS,
provincial hospitals - charge substantially less than private centres, with
longer waits.

**Suspected TB:** treatment is delivered free through the National Tuberculosis
Programme at government facilities and DOTS centres nationwide. See
[[nepal-tb-programme]].

## Practical advice for the patient

- **Telephone ahead.** Specialist clinics do not run daily at every centre, and
  a wasted 10-hour journey is a serious harm.
- **Carry the actual images**, not only the report - on CD, film or a
  memory stick.
- Bring previous scans for comparison.
- Bring citizenship documents and any health insurance card - these are needed
  to claim scheme benefits. See [[nepal-government-schemes]].
- Ask about the total expected cost before admission.


> **Verify before relying on these figures.** Costs, entitlements and contact
> details in Nepal change frequently. The figures here are indicative, compiled
> on the review date above, and are intended to help a patient plan - not to
> quote as a guaranteed price. Confirm directly with the facility or scheme.
