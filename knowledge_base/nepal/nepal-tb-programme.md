# Tuberculosis Services in Nepal

Directly relevant to brain imaging in Nepal, because **intracranial tuberculoma
is an important cause of a brain lesion here** and is frequently mistaken for
tumour. See [[tuberculoma]].

## The core message for cost-constrained patients

**TB diagnosis and treatment are provided free of charge** through the National
Tuberculosis Programme at government facilities and DOTS centres nationwide.

This changes the calculus for a family weighing whether to pursue a brain
lesion. If the lesion proves tuberculous, the treatment costs nothing - whereas
a neurosurgical pathway may cost several hundred thousand rupees. Establishing
which it is therefore has direct financial as well as clinical consequence, and
is a strong argument for doing the inexpensive investigations first.

## Services available free

- Sputum microscopy and GeneXpert (rapid molecular testing)
- Chest X-ray at government facilities, at low or no cost
- **First-line anti-TB medication**
- Drug-resistant TB treatment through designated centres
- Directly Observed Treatment (DOTS) through local centres
- Contact tracing and preventive therapy

## Where to access

- Government health posts and primary health care centres
- District and provincial hospitals
- DOTS centres, distributed nationwide including in remote districts
- Designated drug-resistant TB treatment centres

## Practical points for CNS TB

- **A chest X-ray is essential** in any patient with a brain lesion and
  constitutional symptoms. It is cheap, fast and widely available, and evidence
  of pulmonary TB substantially strengthens the case for tuberculoma.
- CNS TB requires **considerably longer treatment than pulmonary TB**, often
  with adjunctive steroids, under specialist supervision.
- **Treatment adherence is critical.** Interruption drives drug resistance. The
  DOTS structure exists precisely to support this, and patients should be
  linked to their local centre before leaving the referral hospital.
- Paradoxical enlargement of lesions early in treatment is recognised and does
  not by itself mean failure.
- HIV testing should be offered - co-infection changes management.

## What a health worker should say

> "One of the things this could be is tuberculosis affecting the brain. That
> sounds serious, and it is - but it is treatable with medicine, and the
> medicine is free from the government programme. Before anything expensive, we
> should do a chest X-ray here, which costs very little and tells us a lot."
