# Referral Pathway in Nepal

## The formal structure

```
Community / Female Community Health Volunteer (FCHV)
        |
Health Post / Urban Health Centre
        |
Primary Health Care Centre
        |
District Hospital            <- CT sometimes available
        |
Provincial Hospital          <- CT usually, MRI sometimes
        |
Central / Tertiary Hospital  <- MRI, neurosurgery
   (mainly Kathmandu Valley, BPKIHS Dharan, Pokhara, Bharatpur)
```

Following this chain matters practically: **health insurance claims generally
require proper referral**, and a patient who self-refers to a tertiary private
hospital often cannot claim.

## Where an AI triage tool fits

The intended point of use is at **health post or district hospital level**,
where a scan may have been obtained but specialist reporting is delayed by
days or weeks.

The tool's contribution is prioritisation: identifying which scans should be
escalated immediately versus which can follow the routine pathway. It does not
replace any step in the chain, and it does not authorise bypassing radiological
review.

## Practical referral advice

**When referring, send with the patient:**

- The actual images (CD, film, or memory stick) - not only the report
- Any previous imaging for comparison
- A written referral note: history, examination findings, observations,
  treatment given, and the specific question being asked
- Citizenship certificate and health insurance card
- A contact telephone number

**Telephone ahead.** Specialist clinics do not run every day at every centre.
Confirm the clinic day and that the specialist is present before a family
commits to a 10-hour journey.

**Be explicit about urgency.** "Please review" does not convey the same thing
as "seen today, reducing consciousness, needs urgent CT". State the timeframe
you expect.

## Emergency referral

For an emergency, bypass the chain and go to the nearest facility with CT and
an emergency department.

- **Ambulance: 102**
- Ambulance availability outside urban areas is limited; private vehicle
  transfer is often faster in practice
- Telephone the receiving hospital before departure where possible

## When the patient cannot travel

This is common and should be planned for honestly rather than ignored:

- Determine which investigations are available locally
- Consider whether teleradiology or sending images electronically to a
  specialist is feasible
- Agree explicit symptom triggers that would justify the journey
- Arrange local follow-up at the health post
- Record the plan in writing, in the patient's language

An achievable plan followed is better than an ideal plan abandoned.
