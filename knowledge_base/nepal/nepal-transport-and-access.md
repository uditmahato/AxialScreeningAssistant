# Transport, Distance and Practical Access

Geography is a clinical variable in Nepal. A recommendation that ignores it
will not be followed, and an unfollowed recommendation helps no one.

## The realities

- Neurosurgical and MRI capability is concentrated in the **Kathmandu Valley**,
  with additional centres at **Dharan (BPKIHS)**, **Pokhara**, **Bharatpur**,
  **Biratnagar**, **Bhairahawa** and **Nepalgunj**.
- From remote districts of **Karnali and Sudurpashchim**, reaching an MRI can
  mean **8-12 hours or more**, sometimes beginning with hours on foot.
- **Monsoon** (roughly June to September) causes landslides and road closures
  that can isolate districts for days.
- **Winter** closes high-altitude routes.
- Air ambulance and scheduled flights exist but are beyond most households'
  means.

## What this means clinically

**Lower the threshold for early transfer.** Where transfer takes 10 hours, a
patient who might be observed locally in a well-resourced setting should start
travelling sooner. The deterioration that would prompt transfer elsewhere will
already be advanced by arrival.

**Front-load investigation.** Do everything available locally - chest X-ray,
bloods, glucose, fundoscopy, blood pressure - before the journey, so the
referral centre visit is maximally productive and does not need repeating.

**Consolidate visits.** Arrange scan, specialist review and any tests on the
same trip where possible. Two separate journeys frequently means the second
never happens.

**Plan for the attendant.** Nepali hospital practice expects family to
accompany and assist the patient. That means at least two people's transport,
food and accommodation, and two people's lost earnings.

## Practical checklist before sending a patient

- [ ] Telephone the receiving centre; confirm the clinic runs and the
      specialist is present
- [ ] Written referral note stating the specific question and the urgency
- [ ] All images and previous reports packed
- [ ] Citizenship certificate and insurance card
- [ ] Contact number recorded for both patient and referrer
- [ ] Family briefed on likely total cost, including non-medical costs
- [ ] Red-flag symptoms explained, in the patient's language
- [ ] Local follow-up arranged for after return

## Where travel is genuinely not possible

Be honest and plan accordingly rather than issuing an instruction that will
fail:

- Identify what can be done locally
- Explore sending images electronically for remote specialist opinion
- Agree explicit triggers that would justify the journey despite the difficulty
- Document the plan in the patient's language and leave a copy with the local
  health post
