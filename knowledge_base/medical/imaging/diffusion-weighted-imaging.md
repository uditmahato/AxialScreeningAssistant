# Diffusion Weighted Imaging (DWI)

DWI measures the random movement of water molecules in tissue. Where that
movement is restricted, the tissue appears bright on DWI and dark on the
corresponding ADC map. It answers two questions that no other sequence answers
as well.

## Question 1 - Is this an acute stroke?

Cell death causes cells to swell, trapping water inside them and restricting
its movement. DWI detects this **within minutes** of the arterial occlusion,
long before any change appears on T2, FLAIR or CT.

This makes DWI the single most important sequence in acute stroke imaging, and
it also dates the infarct: restriction appears immediately, persists for
approximately a week to ten days, then fades.

## Question 2 - Is this an abscess or a necrotic tumour?

This distinction is clinically decisive and is often impossible on other
sequences.

- **Pyogenic abscess**: pus is thick and cellular. The **central cavity
  restricts markedly** - bright on DWI, dark on ADC.
- **Necrotic tumour**: the necrotic centre is liquefied debris and **does not
  restrict**. Any restriction is in the solid, cellular portions.

Given that in Nepal the differential for a ring-enhancing lesion prominently
includes infection, DWI carries particular weight here. See
[[ring-enhancing-lesion-differential]].

## Other uses

- **Lymphoma and medulloblastoma** restrict because they are densely cellular
- **Epidermoid cyst** restricts, whereas an arachnoid cyst does not - the
  distinguishing feature between them
- Encephalitis, some demyelinating lesions and acute injury show restriction

## The T2 shine-through pitfall

A lesion that is very bright on T2 can appear bright on DWI without any true
restriction - the brightness "shines through" from the T2 signal. **The ADC map
must always be checked**: true restriction is bright on DWI *and* dark on ADC.
Relying on DWI alone is a common and consequential error.

## Relevance to this system

DWI information is not available to the Axial Screening Assistant, which analyses a single
structural image. This is a real limitation: the system cannot distinguish
abscess from necrotic tumour, and that distinction changes management
completely.

The advisory output therefore presents infection and tumour as parallel
possibilities and directs the user to obtain DWI as part of specialist review,
rather than implying a distinction it cannot make.
