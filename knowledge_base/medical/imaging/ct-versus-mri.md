# CT or MRI - Choosing the Right Scan

The two are not interchangeable, and in a resource-limited setting choosing
correctly the first time matters considerably - both for the patient's outcome
and for their cost.

## Where CT is the correct first choice

- **Acute head injury** - fast, detects skull fracture and acute blood
- **Suspected acute haemorrhage or stroke** - CT excludes bleeding, which is
  the decision that determines whether clot-dissolving treatment is safe
- **Suspected subarachnoid haemorrhage** - highly sensitive in the first hours
- **Any unstable or uncooperative patient** - the scan takes seconds
- **Patients with pacemakers or certain metallic implants**
- **Where MRI is unavailable, or the wait would be clinically unacceptable**

CT advantages: fast, widely available in Nepal including outside Kathmandu,
substantially cheaper, and better for bone and acute blood.

## Where MRI is the correct choice

- **Suspected tumour** - far superior soft-tissue characterisation
- **Suspected infection or inflammation**
- **Posterior fossa and brainstem** - CT is significantly degraded by bone
  artefact in this region
- **Suspected demyelination**
- **Epilepsy investigation**
- **Pituitary and skull base**
- **Acute ischaemic stroke where diffusion imaging is needed** to establish the
  extent and age of infarction

MRI advantages: no ionising radiation, much better soft-tissue contrast,
multiple sequences carrying complementary information.

MRI disadvantages: slower, considerably more expensive, less available, and
unsuitable for some patients with implants or severe claustrophobia.

## The practical position in Nepal

MRI centres are concentrated in major urban areas, particularly Kathmandu.
Patients in rural districts may travel 8-12 hours and pay NPR 8,000-15,000 -
frequently more than a month's income - to obtain a scan.

That reality has two consequences:

1. **The scan should be the right one first time.** Sending a patient on a
   12-hour journey for the wrong modality is a serious practical failure.
2. **A CT that answers the question is better than an unobtainable MRI.** Where
   MRI is not realistically accessible, a CT plus good clinical assessment plus
   a chest X-ray will resolve a substantial proportion of cases - particularly
   where the differential includes TB.
