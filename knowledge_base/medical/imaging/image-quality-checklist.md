# Image Quality Checklist Before Upload

Poor input produces unreliable output. This checklist takes under a minute and
prevents the most common causes of a meaningless result.

## Before uploading, check that the image

1. **Is a brain MRI**, not a CT, an X-ray, or a photograph of a report
2. **Is an axial (horizontal cross-section) view** - the system is designed and
   validated for axial images only
3. **Shows the whole head** within the frame, not a cropped portion
4. **Is in focus and not blurred** - a photograph taken of a screen is often
   too blurred to be usable
5. **Is not a photograph of a film held up to a window** - reflections, glare
   and skew badly degrade the result. Photograph a film on a proper light box
   where possible, or export the digital image directly.
6. **Is right side up**
7. **Has no patient identifying information visible** - name, hospital number
   or date of birth in the image corner. If present, crop it out before
   uploading.
8. **Is JPG or PNG format**, under the size limit

## Common problems and their effect

| Problem | Effect on result |
|---|---|
| Photograph of a screen | Moire patterns and glare; unreliable |
| Heavy JPEG compression | Fine texture lost; may mask small lesions |
| Cropped brain | Model sees an incomplete image; unpredictable |
| Sagittal or coronal view | Outside the validated scope; do not rely on output |
| CT rather than MRI | Different tissue contrast entirely; do not rely on output |
| Very dark or bright | Preprocessing may partially correct, but margin is lost |
| Visible patient name | Privacy breach; crop before uploading |

## About the privacy point

The system does not store patient-identifying information, and uploaded images
are deleted automatically. But an image with a name burned into it is
identifying data the moment it leaves the device. Cropping it out is a
30-second step that removes the risk entirely.

## If the image is poor

It is better to obtain a better image than to act on an unreliable result. If
the only available image is a poor-quality photograph of a film, treat any
output as provisional and rely on clinical assessment and formal radiological
review.
