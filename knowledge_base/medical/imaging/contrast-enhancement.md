# Contrast Enhancement Patterns

Gadolinium contrast does not cross an intact blood-brain barrier. Enhancement
therefore indicates that the barrier is disrupted - by tumour, inflammation,
infection or vascular abnormality - or that the tissue has no barrier to begin
with, as in the pituitary gland and dura.

The **pattern** of enhancement carries much of the diagnostic weight.

## Patterns and what they suggest

**Solid, homogeneous** - meningioma, lymphoma in an immunocompetent patient,
schwannoma. Suggests a cellular lesion with uniform barrier disruption.

**Ring (complete)** - a wide differential requiring careful assessment. See
[[ring-enhancing-lesion-differential]]. Rim characteristics matter: thin and
regular favours abscess; thick and irregular favours high-grade tumour.

**Incomplete or "open" ring** - relatively characteristic of demyelination,
with the open portion typically facing grey matter. A genuinely useful sign.

**Nodular** - metastases, granuloma, resolving neurocysticercosis.

**Dural / "tail"** - meningioma classically; also dural metastases and
inflammatory conditions.

**Leptomeningeal** (following the brain surface and sulci) - meningitis,
including tuberculous meningitis, and meningeal spread of tumour. Basal
meningeal enhancement is a hallmark of tuberculous meningitis and is an
important finding in the Nepali context.

**Gyral** - subacute infarct, encephalitis.

**None** - low-grade glioma, an established old infarct, most arachnoid cysts,
calcified neurocysticercosis.

## Important cautions

- **Absence of enhancement does not exclude serious pathology.** Low-grade
  gliomas frequently do not enhance.
- **Steroids reduce enhancement.** A patient given steroids before scanning may
  show markedly less enhancement, and in CNS lymphoma the lesion may vanish
  entirely. See [[cns-lymphoma]].
- Contrast requires adequate renal function and carries a small risk of
  reaction. It is also an additional cost, which matters where the patient is
  paying directly.

## Relevance to automated analysis

Whether a given uploaded image is pre- or post-contrast is usually unknown to
an automated system, and the same lesion looks materially different in each. A
system reasoning from a single image without that context should not attempt to
characterise enhancement, and any output describing enhancement pattern would
be unreliable.
