# First Seizure in an Adult

A first unprovoked seizure in an adult always requires investigation. In Nepal
the differential is materially different from that in Western guidance, and
this document reflects that.

## Immediate management during a seizure

- Protect from injury; do not restrain
- **Do not put anything in the mouth**
- Place in the recovery position once the convulsion stops
- Time the seizure
- **Call for emergency help if it lasts over 5 minutes, if seizures repeat
  without recovery in between, if the person is injured, or if this is a first
  seizure**

## Causes to consider in a Nepali adult

Ordered with local epidemiology in mind rather than Western prevalence:

1. **Neurocysticercosis** - the most common cause of acquired epilepsy in
   endemic regions, and Nepal is endemic. See [[neurocysticercosis]].
2. **Tuberculoma** or tuberculous meningitis. See [[tuberculoma]].
3. Previous stroke or head injury
4. **Brain tumour** - primary or metastatic
5. Metabolic causes - low blood glucose, low sodium, kidney or liver failure
6. Alcohol withdrawal
7. CNS infection - meningitis, encephalitis, abscess
8. Eclampsia in pregnancy - a distinct emergency

Note that the first two entries are **treatable infections**, and that a
Western-derived differential would place tumour considerably higher. Anchoring
on tumour leads to expensive and often unnecessary neurosurgical referral.

## Investigation

- Blood glucose immediately - hypoglycaemia is reversible and commonly missed
- Electrolytes, calcium, renal and liver function
- **Brain imaging** - MRI where available; CT is an acceptable and much more
  accessible first step, and will show calcified cysticercosis well
- **Chest X-ray** - cheap and directly relevant to the TB differential
- HIV testing where appropriate
- Fundoscopy - mandatory before any antiparasitic treatment
- EEG, where accessible

## Important

A single seizure is not epilepsy. The diagnosis of epilepsy requires either two
unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart, or one seizure with a high
recurrence risk established by investigation.

Driving, swimming alone, working at height and operating machinery should be
avoided until the person has been assessed by a clinician.
