# Raised Intracranial Pressure

The skull is a rigid container. Any expanding mass, swelling or fluid
accumulation raises the pressure inside it, and beyond a point the brain is
forced through the openings at its base - herniation - which is rapidly fatal.

## Symptoms

- **Headache**, characteristically worse in the morning, on lying flat, and on
  coughing, sneezing or straining
- **Vomiting**, often without preceding nausea, and often early morning
- **Visual disturbance** - blurring, transient greying of vision on standing
  ("visual obscurations"), or double vision from a sixth nerve palsy
- **Drowsiness** - an ominous sign indicating decompensation
- Neck pain or stiffness

## Signs

- **Papilloedema** on fundoscopy - swelling of the optic disc. Its absence does
  not exclude raised pressure, particularly if the rise has been rapid.
- Reduced Glasgow Coma Scale
- **Cushing's triad** - rising blood pressure, slowing pulse, irregular
  breathing. This is a **late and pre-terminal** sign indicating imminent
  herniation.
- A dilated pupil, particularly one-sided, indicating third nerve compression
- In infants: bulging fontanelle, enlarging head, "sunsetting" eyes

## Critical practical points

**Lumbar puncture is contraindicated** where a mass lesion with raised pressure
is suspected. Removing fluid from below creates a pressure gradient that can
precipitate fatal herniation. Imaging must come first.

**Deterioration can be rapid.** A patient who is drowsy but rousable can become
unrousable within hours.

## Immediate management pending transfer

- Sit the patient up to approximately 30 degrees, head midline
- Maintain airway, breathing and circulation
- Avoid hypotension and hypoxia, both of which worsen secondary brain injury
- Treat fever and seizures
- Urgent transfer to a centre with imaging and neurosurgical capability
- Steroids may be indicated for tumour-related oedema, but **not** where CNS
  lymphoma is suspected before biopsy, and this decision belongs to a clinician

## In the rural Nepali context

Where transfer to Kathmandu takes 8-12 hours, recognising raised pressure early
is the decision that determines outcome. A patient with morning headache,
vomiting and any drowsiness should begin transfer, not await further local
observation.
