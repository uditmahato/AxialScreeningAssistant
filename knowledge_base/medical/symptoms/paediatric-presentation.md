# Brain Abnormality in Children - Presentation

Children present differently from adults, and the symptoms are frequently
attributed to common childhood illness. Delay in diagnosis of childhood brain
tumours is a recognised and persistent problem worldwide.

## Warning features by age

**Infants (under 2)**
- Enlarging head circumference crossing centile lines
- Bulging or tense fontanelle
- "Sunsetting" eyes - persistent downward gaze
- Persistent vomiting, poor feeding, failure to thrive
- Irritability or abnormal lethargy
- Loss of previously acquired skills - **developmental regression is always
  significant**

**Older children**
- Persistent morning headache, especially with vomiting
- Vomiting that relieves the headache
- Unsteadiness, frequent falls, clumsiness
- Squint or double vision
- Head tilt - can indicate a posterior fossa lesion
- Deteriorating school performance or behaviour change
- Growth failure or delayed puberty - suggests a lesion near the pituitary
- New seizures

## The most common error

Morning headache with vomiting in a child is very frequently diagnosed as
gastroenteritis or migraine. The distinguishing features are:

- Vomiting that **relieves** the headache
- Symptoms present on waking, improving during the day
- Absence of diarrhoea
- Progression over weeks
- Any accompanying unsteadiness, squint or behaviour change

**A child with recurrent morning headache and vomiting over more than a couple
of weeks needs examination including fundoscopy and consideration of imaging.**

## Common paediatric brain tumours

- [[medulloblastoma]] - posterior fossa, midline
- Pilocytic astrocytoma - often cerebellar, and frequently curable by surgery
- [[ependymoma]] - fourth ventricle
- Brainstem glioma
- [[craniopharyngioma]] - growth failure, visual loss, excessive thirst

## Note on this system's scope

The Axial Screening Assistant is developed and validated on adult axial MRI data. Paediatric
brain anatomy differs substantially - myelination changes appearance markedly
through the first two years - and the tumour distribution is different.
**Outputs on paediatric images should be treated as unvalidated.** A child with
any warning feature needs clinical assessment regardless of what the system
reports.
