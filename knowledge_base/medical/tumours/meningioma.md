# Meningioma

Meningiomas arise from the meninges - the membranes covering the brain - rather
than from brain tissue itself. They are the most common primary intracranial
tumour, and the large majority are benign (WHO grade 1).

Because they grow outside the brain and push on it rather than infiltrating it,
many are curable by surgery, and small asymptomatic ones are often simply
monitored.

## Typical MRI features

- A well-defined, rounded mass attached to a dural surface
- **Extra-axial** - it sits outside the brain and displaces it, rather than
  arising within it. A visible cerebrospinal fluid cleft between mass and brain
  is a key sign.
- Strong, uniform enhancement after contrast
- A "dural tail" - enhancement extending along the adjacent dura - is
  characteristic
- May cause thickening of the overlying skull bone
- Surrounding oedema is variable and does not reliably indicate grade

## Typical presentation

Many are found incidentally on a scan performed for another reason. When
symptomatic, features reflect location and the slow rate of growth:

- Headache
- Seizures
- Gradual weakness or numbness
- Visual disturbance if near the optic pathways
- Personality change with frontal lesions

## What happens next

Neurosurgical review. Management depends on size, location, symptoms and the
patient's age and general health:

- **Small and asymptomatic**: often observed with interval MRI
- **Symptomatic or growing**: surgical removal, which is frequently curative
- **Inaccessible or residual**: radiotherapy may be considered

A meningioma is generally a more favourable finding than an intra-axial tumour,
but it still requires specialist assessment - location matters as much as
grade, and some sites are surgically difficult.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
