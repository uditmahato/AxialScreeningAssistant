# Ependymoma

A tumour arising from ependymal cells, which line the ventricles and the
central canal of the spinal cord. It occurs in both children and adults, with
different typical locations in each.

## Typical MRI features

- Arises within or adjacent to a ventricle
- In children, most often in the fourth ventricle at the back of the brain
- In adults, more often in the spinal cord or the lateral ventricles
- Heterogeneous signal with cysts, calcification and sometimes bleeding
- Variable enhancement
- A characteristic "plastic" behaviour - the tumour squeezes out through the
  openings of the fourth ventricle rather than displacing structures

## Typical presentation

Because these tumours obstruct the flow of cerebrospinal fluid, they commonly
present with raised intracranial pressure:

- Headache, worse in the morning and on lying flat
- Vomiting, often without nausea
- Unsteadiness and clumsiness
- In infants, an enlarging head circumference
- Neck stiffness or head tilt with fourth ventricular tumours

## What happens next

Urgent neurosurgical referral. Hydrocephalus may need to be relieved before
definitive surgery. Management involves maximal safe resection - the extent of
removal is among the most important factors in outcome - usually followed by
radiotherapy. Imaging of the whole spine is required, because these tumours can
spread through the cerebrospinal fluid.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
