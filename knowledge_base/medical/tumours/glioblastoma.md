# Glioblastoma (WHO Grade 4)

Glioblastoma is the most aggressive and the most common malignant primary brain
tumour in adults. Under the 2021 WHO classification the term is reserved for
IDH-wildtype grade 4 astrocytic tumours.

**Imaging cannot confirm this diagnosis.** The appearance overlaps with brain
abscess, tuberculoma and other treatable conditions. Tissue diagnosis is
required.

## Typical MRI features

- A large, irregular mass with a necrotic (dying) centre
- Thick, irregular rim enhancement after contrast
- Extensive surrounding oedema, often disproportionate to the mass itself
- Marked mass effect, sometimes with midline shift
- May cross the corpus callosum to the opposite hemisphere - the classic
  "butterfly" pattern, which is highly suggestive though not exclusive

## Typical presentation

Symptoms usually develop over weeks rather than months, and progress:

- Progressive headache, often worse in the morning
- New focal neurological deficit - weakness, speech disturbance, visual loss
- Seizures
- Cognitive or personality change, frequently noticed first by family

## What happens next

Urgent neurosurgical referral. Management is decided by a multidisciplinary
team and usually combines maximal safe surgical resection, radiotherapy and
chemotherapy. Molecular testing (including MGMT promoter methylation status)
guides the specific plan.

## Distinguishing features on imaging

Radiological features that favour glioblastoma over abscess or infection
include a thick and irregular enhancing rim, and restricted diffusion confined
to the solid portions rather than the cavity. A brain abscess more typically
shows a thin, smooth, regular rim with marked restricted diffusion within the
central cavity. This distinction requires specialist radiological review and
diffusion-weighted imaging - it cannot be made on a single axial slice.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
