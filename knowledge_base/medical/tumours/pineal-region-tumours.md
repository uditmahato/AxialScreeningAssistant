# Pineal Region Tumours

Tumours arising in or around the pineal gland, at the back of the third
ventricle. Uncommon, but important because they obstruct cerebrospinal fluid
flow early and because some are highly treatable.

## Types

- **Germ cell tumours** - germinoma is the most common. Notably **highly
  sensitive to radiotherapy and chemotherapy**, with good outcomes, so making
  the distinction matters greatly.
- **Pineal parenchymal tumours** - pineocytoma (slow-growing) through
  pineoblastoma (aggressive, more common in children)
- **Glial tumours** arising from adjacent structures
- **Pineal cyst** - very common, usually incidental and harmless

## Typical MRI features

- A mass in the pineal region, behind the third ventricle
- Frequently obstructs the cerebral aqueduct, causing hydrocephalus
- Germinomas typically enhance strongly and uniformly
- Calcification patterns can be informative: germ cell tumours tend to engulf
  pineal calcification, while pineal parenchymal tumours disperse it

## Characteristic presentation

**Parinaud's syndrome** - a distinctive triad from compression of the dorsal
midbrain:

- Inability to look upward
- Pupils that react poorly to light but normally to accommodation
- Convergence-retraction nystagmus on attempted upgaze

Together with headache and vomiting from hydrocephalus, this combination is
highly localising.

## What happens next

Urgent neurosurgical referral. Hydrocephalus may need relief first.
Investigation includes tumour markers in blood and cerebrospinal fluid
(alpha-fetoprotein and beta-hCG), which can establish a germ cell diagnosis
without biopsy and directly determine treatment.

Spinal imaging is required, as these tumours can spread through cerebrospinal
fluid.
