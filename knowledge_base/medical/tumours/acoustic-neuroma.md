# Acoustic Neuroma (Vestibular Schwannoma)

A benign tumour of the Schwann cells surrounding the vestibulocochlear nerve
(cranial nerve VIII), which carries hearing and balance information. It grows
slowly in the internal auditory canal and the angle between the cerebellum and
the pons.

## Typical MRI features

- A mass centred on the internal auditory canal
- Frequently an "ice cream cone" shape - a rounded portion in the
  cerebellopontine angle with a narrow extension into the canal
- Strong enhancement after contrast
- Widening of the internal auditory canal
- Larger lesions compress the brainstem and cerebellum

These lesions are easily missed on standard axial brain sequences. Dedicated
thin-slice imaging through the internal auditory canals is needed.

## Typical presentation

Symptoms are typically gradual and one-sided:

- **Progressive hearing loss on one side** - the most common presenting feature
- Ringing in one ear (tinnitus)
- Unsteadiness, particularly in the dark
- Numbness or tingling of the face if the trigeminal nerve is involved
- Facial weakness is a late feature

**One-sided hearing loss in an adult warrants investigation.** It is easy to
attribute to age or wax, and this is the diagnosis most often delayed as a
result.

## What happens next

Referral to ENT and neurosurgery, with formal audiometry. Management options
are observation with interval scanning for small, stable tumours; stereotactic
radiosurgery; or microsurgical removal. Choice depends on size, hearing status,
age and rate of growth.

Bilateral acoustic neuromas are strongly associated with neurofibromatosis type
2 and warrant genetic assessment.
