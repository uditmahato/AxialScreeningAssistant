# Primary CNS Lymphoma

A non-Hodgkin lymphoma confined to the brain, spinal cord, eyes or meninges,
without systemic disease. It is strongly associated with immunosuppression,
particularly advanced HIV infection.

## Typical MRI features

- Lesions adjacent to the ventricles or in the deep structures - basal ganglia,
  corpus callosum, periventricular white matter
- Densely cellular, so it appears dark on T2 relative to other tumours and
  shows restricted diffusion
- **Homogeneous, intense enhancement** in immunocompetent patients
- In immunosuppressed patients, ring enhancement with central necrosis is
  common, which makes it far harder to distinguish from toxoplasmosis or abscess
- Oedema is usually less than would be expected for the lesion size

## Critical caution regarding steroids

Steroids can cause CNS lymphoma lesions to shrink dramatically or disappear
within days - the historical term is "ghost tumour". This destroys the
diagnostic yield of a subsequent biopsy and can delay diagnosis by months.

**Where CNS lymphoma is suspected, steroids should be withheld until tissue
diagnosis has been obtained, unless there is life-threatening mass effect.**
This decision belongs to the treating specialist and is one of the most
important management points in neuro-oncology.

## Typical presentation

- Cognitive or personality change, often prominent
- Focal neurological deficit
- Symptoms of raised intracranial pressure
- Visual symptoms if the eye is involved
- Seizures are less common than with other brain tumours

## What happens next

Urgent referral to neurology, neurosurgery and haemato-oncology. Investigation
includes HIV testing, ophthalmological assessment, cerebrospinal fluid
examination and staging to exclude systemic lymphoma. Management is primarily
with chemotherapy rather than surgery.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
