# Medulloblastoma

A malignant embryonal tumour of the cerebellum, and one of the most common
malignant brain tumours of childhood. It occurs predominantly in children,
though adult cases occur.

## Typical MRI features

- A mass in the posterior fossa, usually arising in the cerebellar vermis in
  the midline
- Frequently fills and obstructs the fourth ventricle, causing hydrocephalus
- Densely cellular, which produces characteristic restricted diffusion
- Variable enhancement
- Spread through the cerebrospinal fluid is common at diagnosis, so imaging of
  the entire spine is mandatory

## Typical presentation

The history is often short - weeks rather than months:

- Morning headache and vomiting
- Unsteadiness, wide-based gait, frequent falls
- Clumsiness of the hands
- In young children, irritability, lethargy and enlarging head
- Squint or double vision

**A child with morning headache, vomiting and unsteadiness needs urgent imaging
and referral.** These features are commonly attributed to gastrointestinal
illness, and that misattribution is the usual cause of delay.

## What happens next

Emergency neurosurgical referral. Hydrocephalus may require urgent treatment.
Management combines surgery, craniospinal radiotherapy (with age restrictions
in very young children) and chemotherapy, guided by molecular subgroup.

Outcomes for medulloblastoma have improved substantially with modern
risk-adapted treatment, which is why prompt referral matters.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
