# Incidental Findings on Brain MRI

An incidental finding is an abnormality discovered on a scan performed for an
unrelated reason. They are common - studies of healthy volunteers find
incidental abnormalities in a meaningful minority of scans - and most require
no treatment.

This matters for an automated triage system, because a scan flagged as
"abnormal" is not automatically a scan that requires urgent intervention.

## Common incidental findings

- **Small meningioma** - often observed rather than treated
- **Arachnoid cyst** - a benign fluid collection, usually present from birth
- **Pineal cyst** - very common, almost always harmless when small
- **Cavum septum pellucidum** - a normal anatomical variant
- **Small vessel white matter changes** - common with increasing age and with
  high blood pressure
- **Empty sella** - often of no significance
- **Developmental venous anomaly** - a normal variant of venous drainage, not a
  malformation requiring treatment

## Why this matters

Two opposite harms follow from mishandling incidental findings:

1. **Overtreatment and anxiety.** Investigating a harmless finding causes
   distress, cost and sometimes procedural risk. In a setting where a family
   may borrow money to fund investigation, this is a serious harm, not a
   theoretical one.
2. **Dismissal.** Assuming a finding is incidental without proper review can
   miss something that mattered.

The resolution is not to guess but to obtain radiological review. What an
automated system should do is flag the abnormality clearly, state that it
cannot determine significance, and route the scan to a clinician.

## In the Nepali context

Where a patient has travelled 8-12 hours and paid a significant share of a
month's income for a scan, the cost of an unnecessary onward referral is
substantial. Communicating that a finding is likely incidental - while still
arranging appropriate review - is a genuine clinical skill, and a system that
cries wolf on every variant will quickly and rightly be ignored.
