# Astrocytoma

Astrocytomas arise from astrocytes, star-shaped supporting cells of the brain.
They span a wide range of behaviour, from slow-growing and potentially curable
to rapidly aggressive.

## Grading

The WHO grade describes how the cells behave, not how large the tumour is:

- **Grade 1** (e.g. pilocytic astrocytoma) - slow-growing, well-circumscribed,
  most common in children and young adults. Often curable by complete surgical
  removal.
- **Grade 2** (diffuse astrocytoma) - slow-growing but infiltrates surrounding
  brain, so complete removal is difficult.
- **Grade 3** (anaplastic astrocytoma) - more rapidly growing.
- **Grade 4** - see [[glioblastoma]].

Since 2021, IDH mutation status is part of the classification and materially
affects how these tumours are grouped and managed.

## Typical MRI features

- Lower grades: a relatively uniform mass, bright on T2/FLAIR, with little or
  no contrast enhancement and limited surrounding oedema
- Higher grades: heterogeneous signal, patchy or ring enhancement, more
  surrounding oedema, and mass effect
- Calcification is uncommon, which helps distinguish these from
  oligodendroglioma

## Typical presentation

Lower-grade tumours often present with seizures alone in an otherwise well
adult, sometimes after years of subtle symptoms. Higher-grade tumours present
more like [[glioblastoma]], with progressive deficit over weeks.

## What happens next

Neurosurgical referral. Even a low-grade appearance warrants specialist review,
because grading cannot be established from imaging alone and lower-grade
tumours can transform over time. Management may involve surgery, radiotherapy,
chemotherapy, or - for some small, asymptomatic low-grade lesions - careful
surveillance imaging.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
