# Craniopharyngioma

A benign but locally troublesome tumour arising from remnants of Rathke's
pouch, near the pituitary gland and the base of the brain. It has two peaks of
incidence: children aged 5-14, and adults over 50.

## Typical MRI features

- A mass in the suprasellar region, above the pituitary gland
- **Characteristically mixed cystic and solid**
- Calcification is common, especially in the childhood form
- Cyst contents are often bright on T1 because of high protein and cholesterol
  content - historically described as "machine oil" fluid
- Enhancement of the solid portions and the cyst wall

## Typical presentation

Because of where it sits, it affects three neighbouring structures:

- **Optic chiasm** - visual field loss, reduced acuity
- **Pituitary and hypothalamus** - growth failure in children, delayed puberty,
  diabetes insipidus (excessive thirst and urination), fatigue, weight change,
  disordered temperature regulation
- **Third ventricle** - hydrocephalus with headache and vomiting

Growth failure in a child, combined with visual symptoms or excessive thirst,
is a pattern that warrants imaging.

## What happens next

Referral to neurosurgery, endocrinology and ophthalmology. Full pituitary
hormone assessment and formal visual fields are required before any surgery.

Management is a genuine balance: complete removal risks permanent hypothalamic
and pituitary damage, while incomplete removal carries a high recurrence rate.
Many centres favour a planned partial resection followed by radiotherapy.
Lifelong hormone replacement and endocrine follow-up are common.
