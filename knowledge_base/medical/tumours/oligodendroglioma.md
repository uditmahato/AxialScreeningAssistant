# Oligodendroglioma

A glioma arising from oligodendrocytes, the cells that produce the myelin
sheath. Under the 2021 WHO classification the diagnosis requires both an IDH
mutation and 1p/19q co-deletion - it can no longer be made on appearance alone.

## Typical MRI features

- A mass most often in the frontal lobe
- Involves both grey and white matter, frequently reaching the cortical surface
- **Calcification is characteristic** and is present in the majority of cases
- Heterogeneous signal; enhancement is variable
- Often relatively well-defined compared with other diffuse gliomas

## Typical presentation

Seizures are the presenting symptom in a majority of patients, and may precede
diagnosis by a considerable period. Other features depend on location and
include headache and gradual cognitive or personality change.

## Why the molecular classification matters

Tumours with the defining 1p/19q co-deletion respond notably better to
combined chemotherapy and radiotherapy than other diffuse gliomas. This is one
of the clearest examples in neuro-oncology of molecular testing changing
management, and it is why tissue diagnosis is not optional.

## What happens next

Neurosurgical referral for tissue diagnosis and molecular testing. Management
typically involves surgery followed by radiotherapy and chemotherapy, though
timing varies and some patients are observed initially.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
