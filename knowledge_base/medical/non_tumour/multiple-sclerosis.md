# Multiple Sclerosis and Demyelination

An immune-mediated condition in which the myelin sheath around nerve fibres in
the central nervous system is damaged, producing scattered areas of
demyelination.

## Typical MRI features

- Multiple lesions, bright on T2 and FLAIR
- **Periventricular**, typically oriented perpendicular to the ventricles -
  the appearance historically called Dawson's fingers
- Juxtacortical, infratentorial and spinal cord lesions
- Active lesions enhance after contrast; an **incomplete or "open ring"** of
  enhancement is characteristic of demyelination and helps distinguish it from
  tumour and abscess
- Older lesions do not enhance

Diagnosis under the McDonald criteria requires demonstrating lesions
disseminated in **space** (different regions) and in **time** (new lesions on
follow-up, or simultaneous enhancing and non-enhancing lesions).

## Typical presentation

Symptoms depend on lesion location and characteristically evolve over days then
partly recover:

- Optic neuritis - painful visual loss in one eye, often the first episode
- Numbness or tingling
- Weakness
- Unsteadiness, tremor
- Double vision
- Bladder disturbance
- Fatigue, which is frequently the most disabling symptom

## Important cautions

Not every white matter lesion is multiple sclerosis. Small vessel ischaemic
change, migraine-associated lesions, vasculitis and infection can all produce
scattered T2 hyperintensities. Age, distribution, clinical history and follow-up
imaging are all needed to distinguish them, and over-diagnosis from imaging
alone is a recognised problem.

Multiple sclerosis is less common in South Asian populations than in Northern
European ones, so in Nepal the prior probability for scattered white matter
lesions shifts toward infective and vascular causes.

## What happens next

Neurology referral. Investigation may include MRI of the whole neuraxis with
contrast, cerebrospinal fluid examination for oligoclonal bands, visual evoked
potentials, and blood tests to exclude mimics. Management involves treatment of
acute relapses, disease-modifying therapy, and symptom management.
