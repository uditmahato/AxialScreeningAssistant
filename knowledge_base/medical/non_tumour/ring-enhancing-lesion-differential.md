# Ring-Enhancing Brain Lesion - Differential Diagnosis

A ring-enhancing lesion - a mass with a rim that brightens after contrast and a
darker centre - is one of the most common patterns on brain MRI, and one of the
most frequently misinterpreted. **The pattern itself is not specific.**

## Why the order of the differential must change in Nepal

Most published differentials are written for Western populations, where tumour
and metastasis dominate. In Nepal, infective causes are far more prevalent, and
the order should reflect that. Getting this wrong sends a patient with a
treatable infection down a neurosurgical pathway they may not be able to
afford.

## Differential, weighted for a Nepali adult population

1. **Neurocysticercosis** - especially with multiple small lesions, a visible
   scolex, or in a patient presenting with a first seizure. See
   [[neurocysticercosis]].
2. **Tuberculoma** - especially with a dark T2 centre, evidence of TB
   elsewhere, or constitutional symptoms. See [[tuberculoma]].
3. **Pyogenic brain abscess** - a thin, smooth, regular rim with **marked
   restricted diffusion in the cavity**. Look for a source: ear, sinus, dental,
   or cardiac. See [[brain-abscess]].
4. **Primary brain tumour**, particularly glioblastoma - thick, irregular rim;
   restricted diffusion in the solid parts rather than the cavity; extensive
   oedema. See [[glioblastoma]].
5. **Metastases** - often multiple, at the grey-white junction, oedema out of
   proportion to size, and usually a known primary. See [[brain-metastases]].
6. **Toxoplasmosis** - in advanced HIV; often basal ganglia, may be multiple.
7. **Demyelination** - an incomplete or "open ring" of enhancement, typically
   open toward the grey matter, is characteristic of a demyelinating lesion.
8. **Resolving haematoma** or subacute infarct.

## Features that genuinely help discriminate

| Feature | Points toward |
|---|---|
| Scolex visible within cyst | Neurocysticercosis (near-diagnostic) |
| Dark centre on T2 | Tuberculoma |
| Marked restricted diffusion **in the cavity** | Pyogenic abscess |
| Thick, irregular, nodular rim | High-grade tumour |
| Thin, smooth, regular rim | Abscess or infection |
| Multiple lesions, grey-white junction, known cancer | Metastases |
| Incomplete "open ring" | Demyelination |
| Fever, weight loss, night sweats | Infection - TB in particular |
| Abnormal chest X-ray | Tuberculoma or metastases |

## Practical first steps in a resource-limited setting

Before any expensive onward referral, several cheap investigations
substantially reshape the differential:

- **Chest X-ray** - low cost, widely available, and directly informative for
  both TB and lung metastases
- **HIV test**
- **Fundoscopy** - mandatory before antiparasitic treatment for suspected
  neurocysticercosis
- Full blood count, ESR/CRP
- A careful history: seizure, fever, weight loss, TB contact, known cancer,
  ear or dental infection

## What an automated system should and should not say

A classifier can report that a scan appears **abnormal**. It should present the
differential as possibilities weighted for the local population, state clearly
that imaging alone cannot distinguish between them, and direct the user to
appropriate specialist review and the inexpensive investigations above.

It must not name a single diagnosis.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
