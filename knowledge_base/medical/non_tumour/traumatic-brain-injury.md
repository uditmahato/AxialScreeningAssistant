# Traumatic Brain Injury

Injury to the brain from an external force. A major cause of death and
disability, particularly in young adults, and heavily represented in road
traffic injury.

## Primary and secondary injury

**Primary injury** occurs at the moment of impact and cannot be reversed.

**Secondary injury** develops over the following hours and days - from swelling,
bleeding, low blood pressure, low oxygen, seizures and fever. **This is what
treatment prevents**, and it is why early management matters so much even
though the initial injury is fixed.

## Patterns on imaging

- **Extradural haematoma** - lens-shaped, does not cross sutures. See
  [[extradural-haematoma]].
- **Subdural haematoma** - crescent-shaped, crosses sutures. See
  [[subdural-haematoma]].
- **Contusion** - bruising of brain tissue, typically at the frontal and
  temporal poles where the brain strikes bone
- **Diffuse axonal injury** - shearing of white matter tracts from rotational
  force. Often **strikingly normal on CT despite profound coma** - MRI with
  gradient-echo or SWI is far more sensitive.
- **Traumatic subarachnoid haemorrhage**
- Skull fracture

## When to image after head injury

Urgent CT is indicated for any of:

- Reduced consciousness (GCS below 15 at 2 hours, or below 13 at any point)
- Suspected open or depressed skull fracture
- Signs of skull base fracture - bruising around the eyes or behind the ears,
  blood or clear fluid from the ear or nose
- Post-traumatic seizure
- Focal neurological deficit
- More than one episode of vomiting
- Anticoagulant use
- Dangerous mechanism of injury

## Critical warning - the lucid interval

A patient who was knocked out, then woke and seemed well, can deteriorate
rapidly hours later from an extradural haematoma. **"He seems fine now" is not
reassurance after a significant head injury.**

Anyone sent home after head injury, and their family, must be given clear
instructions to return immediately if there is increasing drowsiness, worsening
headache, repeated vomiting, weakness, seizure or confusion.

## In the rural Nepali context

Where transfer to a CT-capable centre takes many hours, the threshold for
starting that journey should be low. The features above justify immediate
transfer rather than local observation, because the treatable causes of
deterioration - particularly extradural haematoma - are time-critical and have
excellent outcomes when operated on promptly.
