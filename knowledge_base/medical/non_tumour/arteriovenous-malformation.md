# Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM)

A congenital tangle of abnormal vessels in which arteries connect directly to
veins without an intervening capillary bed. The high-pressure, high-flow
connection predisposes to bleeding.

## Typical MRI features

- A tangle ("nidus") of **flow voids** - dark serpiginous structures on T1 and
  T2, caused by rapidly flowing blood
- Enlarged feeding arteries and draining veins
- Surrounding gliosis or evidence of previous bleeding, including haemosiderin
- Usually little mass effect unless there has been haemorrhage
- Best characterised by catheter angiography

The flow-void appearance is distinctive and is one of the few vascular lesions
readily recognised on standard MRI sequences.

## Typical presentation

- **Haemorrhage** - the most common and most serious presentation
- Seizures
- Headache
- Progressive neurological deficit from "steal" of blood from adjacent brain
- A pulsatile bruit, occasionally audible

## What happens next

Referral to a neurovascular service. Management is genuinely complex and
individualised: options include observation, surgical resection, endovascular
embolisation, stereotactic radiosurgery, or a combination.

The decision weighs the annual bleeding risk against the risk of intervention.
Notably, evidence from randomised trial data suggests that for *unruptured*
AVMs, conservative management may produce better outcomes than intervention in
some patient groups - so this is not a lesion where treatment is automatic.
