# Intracranial Tuberculoma

**A high-priority diagnosis in the Nepali context.**

A tuberculoma is a mass of tuberculous granulation tissue within the brain.
Nepal has a substantial tuberculosis burden, and central nervous system
involvement, while a minority of TB cases, is disproportionately serious.

Like neurocysticercosis, this is a **treatable** condition that is readily
mistaken for a tumour on imaging - with the same consequence if missed:
unnecessary neurosurgical referral and cost, and delayed treatment of a
curable infection.

## Typical MRI features

- Single or multiple rounded lesions
- **Ring enhancement**, classically with a thick and somewhat irregular wall
- A characteristic feature is a lesion appearing **dark (hypointense) on T2**
  in its centre, reflecting solid caseating material - unusual among brain
  lesions and a useful discriminator
- Surrounding oedema, often marked
- Common at the grey-white junction and in the posterior fossa
- Associated findings strongly support the diagnosis: basal meningeal
  enhancement, hydrocephalus, and infarcts from vasculitis

## Supporting evidence to look for

The imaging alone is rarely conclusive. The diagnosis is usually built from:

- Evidence of TB elsewhere, especially pulmonary - **a chest X-ray is
  essential and cheap**
- Constitutional symptoms: fever, night sweats, weight loss, prolonged cough
- Known TB contact
- HIV status
- Cerebrospinal fluid examination where safe to perform
- Response to a therapeutic trial of anti-tuberculous therapy, in settings
  where that approach is accepted practice

## Typical presentation

- Headache, often prolonged
- Seizures
- Focal neurological deficit
- Fever and weight loss, though these may be absent
- Features of meningitis if there is concurrent tuberculous meningitis

## What happens next

Referral to medicine, neurology or infectious diseases - **not automatically to
neurosurgery**. Investigation should include chest imaging, HIV testing and
sputum examination.

Management is prolonged anti-tuberculous chemotherapy, considerably longer than
for pulmonary TB, frequently with adjunctive steroids. Treatment is delivered
through Nepal's National Tuberculosis Control Programme, which provides TB
medication free of charge - a materially important point for families facing
cost barriers.

Paradoxical enlargement of lesions during early treatment is well recognised
and does not by itself indicate treatment failure.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
