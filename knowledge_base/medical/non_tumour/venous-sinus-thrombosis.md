# Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis

A clot in the veins or venous sinuses that drain blood from the brain. It is
uncommon, frequently missed, and **treatable** - which makes it one of the most
important diagnoses not to overlook.

## Why it is missed

The presentation is non-specific and does not follow arterial territories. It
most often affects young adults, particularly women, in whom serious
neurological disease is less readily suspected. Headache may be the only
symptom for days.

## Typical imaging features

- Absent flow void in a venous sinus
- The clot itself may be visible as abnormal signal within the sinus
- Venous infarcts that **do not conform to an arterial territory** - a key clue
- Haemorrhagic infarction, often with disproportionate oedema
- Diagnosis usually requires dedicated venography (MRV or CTV)

An infarct that does not fit an arterial territory should prompt consideration
of venous thrombosis.

## Risk factors

- Pregnancy and the postpartum period
- Combined oral contraceptive use
- Dehydration
- Infection, particularly of the ear, sinuses or face
- Inherited or acquired thrombophilia
- Malignancy
- Head injury

## Presentation

- Headache - present in the great majority, often progressive over days
- Seizures, more common than in arterial stroke
- Focal deficit
- Papilloedema and visual obscurations
- Reduced consciousness in severe cases

## What happens next

Urgent hospital referral and dedicated venous imaging. Management is
anticoagulation - **including in the presence of venous haemorrhage**, which is
counterintuitive but is standard practice, because the haemorrhage results from
venous congestion that anticoagulation relieves. Underlying causes are
investigated and treated.

Outcomes with prompt treatment are generally good, which is why recognition
matters.
