# Hydrocephalus

An excess of cerebrospinal fluid within the ventricles, usually because flow is
obstructed or absorption is impaired. It raises pressure within the skull and
can be rapidly fatal if acute.

## Typical imaging features

- Enlarged ventricles, disproportionate to any brain volume loss
- Rounding of the frontal horns
- Enlargement of the temporal horns - an early and sensitive sign
- Periventricular signal change from fluid forced into surrounding tissue
- Effacement of the surface sulci in acute cases

Distinguishing true hydrocephalus from ventricular enlargement secondary to
brain atrophy is a common difficulty. In atrophy the surface sulci are also
enlarged; in hydrocephalus they are compressed.

## Types

- **Obstructive** - a blockage within the ventricular system, for example from
  a tumour of the posterior fossa or aqueduct stenosis
- **Communicating** - impaired absorption, following meningitis, subarachnoid
  haemorrhage, or of unknown cause
- **Normal pressure hydrocephalus** - a distinct condition in older adults with
  the triad of gait disturbance, urinary incontinence and cognitive decline;
  potentially treatable by shunting

## Presentation

**In infants** - enlarging head circumference, bulging fontanelle, separated
sutures, downward deviation of the eyes ("sunsetting"), irritability, poor
feeding.

**In older children and adults** - headache worse in the morning and on lying
flat, vomiting, blurred or double vision, drowsiness, unsteadiness. Papilloedema
on fundoscopy.

**Acute hydrocephalus is an emergency** - rapid deterioration to coma and death
can occur within hours.

## Relevance in Nepal

Post-infective hydrocephalus following tuberculous meningitis or pyogenic
meningitis is an important cause in this setting, as is post-haemorrhagic
hydrocephalus. Both are more common where access to timely treatment of the
underlying infection is limited.

## What happens next

Urgent neurosurgical referral. Treatment addresses the cause where possible and
diverts cerebrospinal fluid - by ventriculoperitoneal shunt or endoscopic third
ventriculostomy. Shunt complications, particularly blockage and infection,
require lifelong awareness: **a patient with a shunt and new headache or
drowsiness needs urgent assessment for shunt malfunction.**
