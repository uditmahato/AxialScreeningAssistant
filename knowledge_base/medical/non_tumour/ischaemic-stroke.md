# Ischaemic Stroke (Cerebral Infarction)

Death of brain tissue caused by interruption of its blood supply. **This is a
time-critical emergency** - the window for treatments that can reverse the
damage is measured in hours.

## The single most important message

**"Time is brain."** Roughly 1.9 million neurons are lost per minute in a large
vessel stroke. Any patient with sudden focal neurological symptoms should reach
a hospital capable of imaging and treating stroke immediately, not after a
period of observation at home.

## Recognition - FAST

- **F**ace - has the face drooped on one side?
- **A**rms - can both arms be raised and held?
- **S**peech - is speech slurred or garbled?
- **T**ime - if any of these are present, seek emergency care immediately

## Typical MRI features by age of infarct

- **Hyperacute (0-6 hours)** - restricted diffusion on DWI is the earliest
  reliable sign, often before any change is visible on other sequences. This is
  why DWI is essential in acute stroke imaging.
- **Acute (6-24 hours)** - bright on T2/FLAIR, some swelling
- **Subacute (days to weeks)** - swelling peaks then subsides; enhancement may
  appear
- **Chronic** - tissue loss, cavity formation, following cerebrospinal fluid
  signal

A key feature is that the abnormality conforms to a **vascular territory** -
the region supplied by a particular artery - rather than crossing anatomical
boundaries as a tumour does.

## Why this matters for an automated triage system

An acute infarct will be classified as "abnormal" by a system trained to
separate normal from abnormal. That output is correct but insufficient - the
urgency of stroke is entirely different from that of a slow-growing tumour. Any
result must therefore be accompanied by red-flag guidance that directs the user
to emergency care on the basis of **symptoms**, independent of what the model
predicted.

## What happens next

Emergency hospital assessment. Depending on time from onset and imaging
findings, treatment may include clot-dissolving therapy or mechanical clot
retrieval, followed by admission to a stroke unit, swallow assessment and
secondary prevention.
