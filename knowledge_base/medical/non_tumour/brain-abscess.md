# Brain Abscess

A collection of pus within the brain, usually bacterial. This is a
**neurosurgical emergency** - untreated, it expands, ruptures into the
ventricles, or causes fatal raised intracranial pressure.

## Typical MRI features

- A rounded lesion with a **thin, smooth, regular enhancing rim** - notably
  more regular than a tumour's rim
- The rim is often thinner on the deep (ventricular) side, which is where
  rupture tends to occur
- **Marked restricted diffusion within the central cavity** - bright on DWI,
  dark on ADC. This is the single most useful discriminating feature from a
  necrotic tumour, where the cavity does not restrict.
- Substantial surrounding oedema
- May be multiple, particularly with blood-borne spread

## Sources to look for

- **Direct spread**: middle ear infection, mastoiditis, sinusitis, dental
  infection - all common and all treatable
- **Blood-borne**: infective endocarditis, lung infection, congenital heart
  disease with right-to-left shunt
- **Penetrating injury** or previous neurosurgery
- **Immunosuppression**, including HIV and diabetes

## Typical presentation

The classic triad of fever, headache and focal deficit is present in only a
minority of patients - **absence of fever does not exclude the diagnosis.**

- Headache, usually persistent and progressive
- Fever, which may be low-grade or absent
- Focal neurological deficit
- Seizures
- Reduced consciousness - a late and serious sign
- Neck stiffness if there is meningeal involvement

## What happens next - urgently

- **Immediate hospital referral**
- Blood cultures before antibiotics where this does not cause delay
- Urgent neurosurgical assessment: many abscesses require aspiration or
  drainage, which is both therapeutic and provides the organism
- Prolonged intravenous antibiotics, typically weeks
- Identification and treatment of the source
- **Lumbar puncture is contraindicated** where there is a mass lesion with
  raised intracranial pressure - it risks fatal herniation

## Why the distinction from tumour matters

An abscess treated as a tumour loses critical days. A tumour treated as an
abscess receives antibiotics that will not help. The diffusion-weighted imaging
finding above is what separates them, and it is why DWI should be part of any
protocol assessing a ring-enhancing lesion.

## Seek emergency care if

- Consciousness is reduced, or the person becomes unusually drowsy or confused
- A seizure occurs, especially a first seizure or one lasting over 5 minutes
- Weakness, numbness or speech difficulty develops or worsens rapidly
- Headache becomes suddenly severe, with repeated vomiting
