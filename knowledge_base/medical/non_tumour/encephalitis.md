# Encephalitis

Inflammation of the brain itself, most often viral. **A neurological emergency**
- outcome depends heavily on how quickly treatment begins.

## Key message

**In suspected viral encephalitis, aciclovir should be started immediately on
clinical suspicion, without waiting for imaging or confirmatory tests.** Herpes
simplex encephalitis is treatable and is frequently fatal or severely disabling
if treatment is delayed.

## Typical presentation

- Fever
- **Altered consciousness or behaviour change** - the defining feature that
  distinguishes encephalitis from meningitis
- Confusion, disorientation, personality change
- Seizures
- Focal neurological deficit
- Headache
- Neck stiffness if there is associated meningitis

## Typical MRI features

- **Herpes simplex**: asymmetric involvement of the temporal lobes and insula,
  bright on T2/FLAIR, often with haemorrhage. Characteristically spares the
  basal ganglia.
- **Japanese encephalitis**: bilateral thalamic involvement - a distinctive
  pattern. **Relevant in Nepal**, particularly the Terai region, where Japanese
  encephalitis is endemic and seasonal.
- Autoimmune encephalitis: medial temporal lobe changes, sometimes with normal
  imaging early on

**Normal imaging early in the illness does not exclude encephalitis.**

## Relevance in Nepal

Japanese encephalitis is endemic in the Terai and transmitted by mosquitoes,
with seasonal peaks during and after the monsoon. A vaccine exists and is part
of the national immunisation programme in affected districts. Acute encephalitis
syndrome is a notifiable condition.

Any patient from an endemic area presenting with fever and altered
consciousness during the season should raise this possibility.

## Investigation and management

- Urgent hospital admission
- Blood glucose, electrolytes, blood cultures
- Lumbar puncture **after** imaging where a mass lesion is suspected
- Cerebrospinal fluid PCR for viruses
- **Empirical aciclovir immediately**
- Supportive care, seizure control, monitoring for raised intracranial pressure
