# Arachnoid Cyst

A benign, fluid-filled sac between the brain and the arachnoid membrane,
usually present from birth. **Most are harmless and require no treatment.**

## Typical MRI features

- A well-defined, smooth-walled collection
- **Follows cerebrospinal fluid signal exactly on every sequence** - the key
  identifying feature
- No enhancement after contrast
- No restricted diffusion, which distinguishes it from an epidermoid cyst
- May remodel adjacent bone or displace brain, without infiltrating it
- Most common in the middle cranial fossa

## Clinical significance

The great majority are incidental findings of no consequence, discovered on a
scan performed for an unrelated reason. Reassurance is usually the correct
management, and this is one of the most important findings for an automated
system to avoid over-escalating.

A minority become clinically relevant when:

- Large enough to cause mass effect
- Obstructing cerebrospinal fluid flow, causing hydrocephalus
- Complicated by bleeding into or around the cyst, particularly after head
  injury
- Associated with seizures attributable to the cyst location

## Presentation when symptomatic

- Headache
- Seizures
- Focal deficit related to location
- In infants, enlarging head or developmental delay
- Sudden deterioration if bleeding occurs

## What happens next

For an asymptomatic incidental cyst: reassurance, with no routine follow-up
imaging required in most cases.

For a symptomatic or large cyst: neurosurgical review. Options include
fenestration (creating a communication with the cerebrospinal fluid spaces) or
shunting.

Patients with large cysts are sometimes advised to avoid contact sports because
of bleeding risk, though the evidence base for this is limited and the advice
should come from the treating neurosurgeon.
