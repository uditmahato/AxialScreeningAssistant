# Axial Screening Assistant

**Brain MRI screening support and bilingual clinical advisory for
resource-limited healthcare settings, built around the clinical realities of
Nepal.**

> **This is a research prototype. It is not a certified medical device, it does
> not provide a diagnosis, and it must never be the sole basis for a clinical
> decision.** Every output carries a disclaimer, and every result requires
> review by a qualified clinician.

---

## The problem

Nepal has **114 neurosurgeons, 0.39 per 100,000 people**: roughly one per
256,000, against a benchmark of one per 100,000 cited by the American College of
Surgeons and the AANS. Reaching it would take about 178 more neurosurgeons, at a
current training rate of roughly 11 a year. MRI capability is concentrated in the
Kathmandu Valley: a patient in a far-western district may travel 8-12 hours and
pay NPR 8,000-15,000 for a scan, frequently more than a month's income, and then
wait weeks for it to be read.

> **Source note.** A widely repeated figure gives "30-40 neurosurgeons, one per
> 750,000, against a WHO target". Both halves are wrong. The primary source,
> Shrestha (2024), reports 114, and the figures above should be used instead.
> See [docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md#12-a-corrected-workforce-statistic).

This system is built for the gap in the middle: a scan exists, but no
radiologist is available to look at it soon.

## What it does

1. Classifies an axial brain MRI image as **normal** or **abnormal**
2. Shows **where the model looked** (Grad-CAM), so a non-specialist can judge
   whether the prediction deserves any weight
3. Generates **clinical guidance** grounded in a curated 52-document corpus,
   never from the language model's own memory
4. Answers questions in **English and Nepali**: including romanised Nepali
5. Produces a **downloadable PDF report** to send with a referral

## What makes it specific to Nepal

A system built on Western prevalence data would be actively harmful here.

In Nepal, **neurocysticercosis and tuberculoma are among the most common causes
of a focal brain lesion**, and both are routinely mistaken for tumour on
imaging. Both are treatable, one with antiparasitic therapy, the other with
anti-TB medication that the government provides free.

Ranking "glioma" above these for a young patient with a first seizure does not
just give a poor answer. It points a family toward neurosurgical referral and a
cost they may not survive financially, for a condition usually managed with
medication.

The knowledge base is therefore weighted for local epidemiology, the advisory
prompts require infective causes to be presented alongside tumour, and a
dedicated differential document exists for exactly this decision.

duplicate-safe split, single run, seed 42. Full comparison in
`artifacts/comparison/`.

Recall is bolded because it is the number that matters clinically: it is the
fraction of abnormal scans the system flags. Accuracy averages a miss and a
false alarm as though they cost the same, and in triage they do not.

### An important caveat on these numbers

**46% of Br35H images have an exact perceptual duplicate elsewhere in the
dataset**, 1,853 duplicate pairs covering 1,377 images, measured with a
difference hash. Left undetected, a large share of the test set has a twin in
the training set and the resulting accuracy measures memorisation rather than
generalisation.

`neuroscan.data.dedup` detects these and assigns duplicates a shared patient id
so the splitter keeps them together. The figures above are computed **after**
that grouping. They would be meaningfully higher, and meaningfully less
truthful, without it.

This is a concrete instance of the dataset-quality problem the literature
raises about public MRI benchmarks, and it is stated in the limitations.
[docs/RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md](docs/RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md) measures it across
three public datasets.

### On the operating threshold

The threshold is chosen on the validation split by maximising **F2**: not F1,
subject to a 0.95 recall floor. F1 weights precision and recall equally, which
asserts that a missed tumour and a false alarm cost the same; F2 weights recall
twice as heavily.

This is not a cosmetic change. On identical weights and splits, switching the
objective from F1 to F2 meant **27 fewer missed abnormal scans across the three
architectures**, EfficientNetB0 alone went from 17 missed to 4. Two of the
three models improved on accuracy *as well as* recall, which shows the F1
thresholds were not a different trade-off but simply badly placed.

Full numbers in [docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md#6-recall-floor-raised-from-090-to-095).

### The figures to quote: 5-fold cross-validation

Single-split numbers are not stable here, repartitioning once moved
baseline_cnn's tuned threshold from 0.535 to 0.160 and its result from *4
missed, 2 false alarms* to *0 missed, 41*. The weights are stable (AUC barely
moves); the operating point, fit on one ~450-image validation split, is not.
So the defensible headline is the cross-validated mean, with each fold using
its own independently tuned threshold:

| Metric (efficientnet_b0, 5 folds) | Mean ± sd | Fold range |
|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | **0.9733 ± 0.0128** | 0.9552-0.9912 |
| **Recall (abnormal)** | **0.9895 ± 0.0082** | 0.9778-1.0000 |
| Precision | 0.9613 ± 0.0212 | 0.9402-0.9955 |
| F1 | 0.9751 ± 0.0117 | 0.9586-0.9910 |
| AUC-ROC | 0.9970 ± 0.0019 | 0.9941-0.9989 |
| Specificity | 0.9548 ± 0.0245 | 0.9296-0.9956 |

Every fold clears the 90% accuracy target, and recall never drops below
0.978 across five different partitions with five independently tuned
thresholds, which is the direct answer to the threshold-stability concern.
Raw per-fold data: `artifacts/runs/*cv_efficientnet_b0*/cross_validation.json`.

See [docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md#13-the-operating-threshold-is-the-least-stable-part-of-the-system).

## Design decisions worth knowing about

Each of these was a real fork in the road; the reasoning is in the code at the
point of decision.

**PyTorch rather than TensorFlow.** The original design specified
TensorFlow/Keras, but TensorFlow has had no native-Windows GPU support since
2.11. Every *method* is unchanged, custom CNN, VGG16, EfficientNetB0, CLAHE,
Grad-CAM, the same metrics, only the library differs.

**E5 embeddings rather than a paraphrase model.** The obvious multilingual
choice, `paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM`, is trained for *symmetric* sentence
similarity. Retrieval here is *asymmetric*: a four-word question against a
several-hundred-word passage. Measured on this corpus, the paraphrase model
returned only tumour documents for *"could it be an infection rather than
cancer?"*, with the ring-enhancing differential absent from the top ten. E5
returns neurocysticercosis, the differential, DWI and abscess.

**Threshold tuned to a recall floor, and to the middle of the tied plateau.**
`argmax` is a 0.5 threshold, optimal only when errors are symmetric, and here
a miss and a false alarm are nothing like equal. The threshold is selected on
validation against a 0.95 recall floor. When several thresholds tie, the
*midpoint* is chosen rather than an endpoint: an endpoint sits flush against a
validation score with zero margin, and in testing that cost 10 points of
accuracy on unseen data.

**Near-duplicate grouping before splitting.** See the caveat above.

**Nepali rendered to images in PDFs.** ReportLab performs no complex text
shaping, so Devanagari comes out with reordered vowel signs and broken
conjuncts, visibly wrong to any Nepali reader. Nepali passages are rendered
through Pillow, which applies the font's own layout tables, and embedded as
images. The cost is that Nepali text is not selectable; correctness is worth
more.

**The system starts even when parts are missing.** No Ollama server means the
advisory shows retrieved source text verbatim rather than failing. For a clinic
machine, degraded is far better than down.

## Safety design

| Mechanism | Where |
|---|---|
| Non-dismissible disclaimer on every page | `web/templates/base.html` |
| Question screening **before** retrieval | `safety.REFUSAL_TRIGGERS` |
| Output validation **after** generation | `rag/advisory.PROHIBITED_PATTERNS` |
| Fixed safe response when retrieval finds nothing | `safety.get_no_context_fallback` |
| Red flags shown regardless of prediction | `safety.get_red_flags` |
| Non-brain-image rejection | `data/preprocessing.is_plausible_brain_scan` |
| Diffuse-attention warning | `explain/gradcam.GradCAMResult.is_diffuse` |
| Uploads purged after 24 hours | `web/services.purge_old_uploads` |

The model never answers from its own knowledge. If retrieval returns nothing
above threshold, generation is skipped entirely, that is the failure mode that
produces confident, fabricated medical advice.

See [docs/ETHICS.md](docs/ETHICS.md).

## Limitations

Stated plainly, because a medical AI trusted beyond its evidence is more
dangerous than none.

- **Single axial slice.** No other slices, planes or sequences. A "normal"
  result does not mean a normal brain.
- **Cannot distinguish infection from tumour.** That needs diffusion-weighted
  imaging, which a single structural image does not provide, and in Nepal this
  is the distinction that matters most.
- **Trained on public data.** The Grande International Hospital clinical
  dataset awaits ethics approval; no clinical training has been performed.
- **Br35H duplication** inflates any figure reported without duplicate
  grouping.
- **Binary only.** Tumour subtype classification is not yet implemented.
- **Adult scans.** Paediatric anatomy differs substantially; outputs on
  paediatric images are unvalidated.
- **Usability study not yet run.** The instrument is complete; the study is
  not.
- **Nepali cost and scheme figures are indicative** and carry review dates.
